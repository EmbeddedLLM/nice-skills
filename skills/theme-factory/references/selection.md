Choose a button colour that belongs to the palette and is recognisable at a small size. Avoid making every button equally vivid. A muted deep blue, olive, plum or burgundy can anchor light palettes; luminous fills can work on dark canvases. Light pastel buttons can also work with dark text and a clear boundary. Never force white text onto a fill just because it is the primary colour.

Use rounded contrast values for display only; use full precision for pass/fail. Aim for at least 4.5:1 for ordinary text, and check visible control boundaries and focus indicators against adjoining surfaces. Do not darken a source colour indiscriminately: preserve its hue where practical and disclose any derived shade.

When asked to improve appearance, assess the full composition: canvas temperature, amount of colour, primary-button emphasis, secondary surfaces and text hierarchy. Popularity provides inspiration, not proof of aesthetic quality or accessibility.
