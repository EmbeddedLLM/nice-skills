# Product research

Use this workflow for purchase recommendations and comparisons. Apply the shared research loop in `../SKILL.md`; the guidance here covers product-specific evidence and decisions.

Forum coverage and evidence handling adapt the permitted Adaptive Deep Research guidance; see [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md) for attribution and modifications.

## Establish what the purchase needs to solve

Translate the request into use situations and success criteria. For "the best headphones," daily calls in a quiet room, travel, and music listening can lead to different choices. Ask about the situations that distinguish the options, drawing on information already supplied.

Clarify consequential gaps such as purchase region, all-in budget, existing equipment, compatibility, frequency of use, and deal-breakers. Separate must-haves from preferences. If a user requests a feature, understand its purpose before recommending a substitute; do not discard an explicit requirement because another product ranks better generally.

## Build and narrow a candidate set

Start with plausible candidates that meet the hard requirements, including user-named options. Record reasons for excluding otherwise credible choices. Keep the comparison broad enough to find alternatives without exhaustively cataloging the market.

Use complementary sources:

| Question | Useful evidence | Limit to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does it support the required feature or setup? | Manufacturer manuals, specifications, compatibility lists, release notes | Exact model, region, hardware revision, firmware, and subscription tier |
| How well does it perform? | Independent comparative tests with disclosed methods | Test conditions and whether they match the user's use |
| What is ownership like over time? | Owner forums, specialist communities, retailer reviews, repair discussions | Ownership duration, self-selection, sponsorship, and duplicate reports |
| What will it cost and how is it supported? | Current regional listings, warranty and return terms, consumable or service pricing | Seller, availability, tax/shipping, recurring costs, and policy date |

Verify changing prices and availability close to delivery when live access is available. Label old or unavailable pricing clearly. Consider total cost and practical burdens that matter to this user: accessories, subscriptions, consumables, maintenance, repairability, learning effort, or switching costs.

## Search beyond indexed reviews

For consequential shortlisted products, seek owner experience as well as specifications and professional reviews. A web search alone may miss community discussions. When search results are sparse, a forum is known to hold relevant experience, or direct text retrieval omits content, use an available authorized browser:

1. Visit the relevant community or product forum and use its native search. Try exact model names, common abbreviations, previous names, and terms such as long-term use, returned, repair, warranty, or a specific suspected problem. Include neutral experience queries so the sample is not entirely complaints.
2. Inspect relevant categories, date filters, thread pages, expanded replies, and follow-up posts. Include relevant local-language communities when the user's region or product market warrants them. Record the query, date window, sort order, and inspected range. Check whether search defaults or sorting hide older ownership reports or newer fixes. Read enough context to distinguish an original fault report, speculation, a workaround, and a confirmed resolution.
3. Record a thread or post permalink when possible, its date, the model/version, use conditions, ownership duration when stated, and the observation relevant to the comparison. Note partial access and which claims could not be checked.
4. If the browser or site is unavailable, try other accessible communities, documented repair evidence, or user-supplied excerpts. Disclose the missing coverage. Request help only when that gap could change the decision; do not infer that the product has no reported problems.

Use existing authorized access only. Do not create accounts, bypass access controls, post questions, or contact reviewers as an implicit part of research. Avoid reproducing unnecessary personal details from community posts.

Use a sufficient authorized connector or API when available; a browser is another retrieval route, not proof that it will expose every reply. Record unresolved pagination, result caps, omitted replies, and partial exports. Reading all returned records does not establish that the whole discussion was read. Preserve reply-to-thread relationships and use observed permalinks instead of inventing source URLs.

## Weigh owner reports without manufacturing consensus

Distinguish firsthand ownership from hearsay, short unboxing impressions from extended use, and independent reports from people repeating the same story. Look for specific conditions, reproducible symptoms, follow-up outcomes, and corroboration across independent sources. Note affiliate incentives, supplied review units, moderation context, or suspicious repetition where observable; do not declare a review fake without evidence.

Forum complaints can identify possible failure modes, but their frequency does not establish a population failure rate. Review scores across sites may reflect different scales, incentives, and populations. Describe recurring observations and uncertainty rather than treating post counts or star averages as controlled measurements.

If the task calls for quantitative review analysis, define the sample unit and denominator first. Deduplicate the inspected records and distinguish separate incidents from replies discussing one incident. Report the collection method and missing coverage with any calculated counts; popularity is not a reliability measure.

Investigate whether apparent disagreements concern different revisions, usage conditions, or expectations. A laboratory test may establish measurable performance while owner reports reveal comfort or maintenance issues; explain both when they affect the user's needs. If a software update reportedly fixes a problem, distinguish the release-note claim from independent confirmation on the relevant version.

## Return to the user at decision points

Ask a follow-up when new evidence makes an unstated preference decisive. Tie the question to the finding and its consequence rather than restarting intake. For example: "Both finalists meet the performance requirement, but one needs frequent manual cleaning. How much maintenance would you accept each week?"

If no candidate meets all hard constraints, identify the conflict and ask which constraint, if any, can change. If the user cannot respond, present conditional options or conclude that none currently qualifies. Do not quietly relax the budget or compatibility requirement to produce a winner.

## Deliver an actionable comparison

Lead with the best-supported choice for the stated use, or explain why no choice is yet justified. Use a compact comparison of finalists against decisive criteria, with source links beside specifications, measured results, and material ownership findings. Separate verified facts from owner observations and your inference.

Explain the strongest alternative, who should avoid each finalist, unresolved concerns, and the few checks that remain before purchase. Where priorities change the winner, give conditional recommendations instead of a universal ranking.

Example: “Find a robot vacuum under 500 units of my local currency.” Clarify region, floor surfaces, pets, and maintenance tolerance if these are unknown. Check local pricing and manuals, inspect independent cleaning tests, and use community search for long-term maintenance reports. If the strongest candidates require different levels of manual upkeep, ask which trade-off fits the household before choosing. If a relevant forum cannot be accessed, say which ownership questions remain uncertain and qualify the recommendation accordingly.
