# Finance decks

For financial reporting, planning, valuation, and investment decisions. A deck that merely contains numbers does not need this guidance. The user's terminology and template govern.

## Claims and status

- State the result, outlook, or decision, then the evidence behind it. Make unmistakable which figures are actuals, which are forecasts, and which are assumptions — and which sentences are recommendations.
- Never present a prepared proposal as approved, or an approved plan as completed.

## Numbers in prose

- When a number carries the point, include the period, what it is compared against, and whatever magnitude the reader needs to interpret it.
- Explain a cause only where the evidence supports the explanation. A target does not by itself say how it will be met.
- A factual result needs no slogan or recommendation bolted on.
- Name the actual result, constraint, risk, or action. Keep precise technical terms and quotations exact. There is no universal banned-word list.

## Units and signs

- Show currency, scale, percentages, and multiples correctly. Do not attach a currency to a count, a year, or a ratio, and do not switch between millions and billions for effect.
- Keep positive and negative signs right. An increase is not automatically good news.

## Consistency and caveats

- Totals, and every number quoted in the text, must agree with the same calculation.
- Where an assumption or an uncertainty affects a decision, it stays on the slide. Supporting detail and citations can go in the notes.
- Keep subtitles and short annotations only when they add information. Do not describe how the deck was made, repeat the headline, or invent a conclusion to fill space.

Chart labels must state the unit and the period, and every number on a slide has to tie back to the workbook it came from. For layout, see the [visual design guidance](../style_guidelines.md); for prose, follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#writing).
