# Document coauthoring

Deliver a complete, coherent draft in the user's requested destination or format. This workflow needs source material and text editing, with no paid writing service or workspace integration.

## Develop the document

1. Read the existing draft, sources, template, and direct user edits. Identify audience, purpose, decision or action, constraints, and unresolved facts. Ask only for consequential missing information; continue drafting supported sections.
2. Separate facts, assumptions, decisions, and open questions. Choose a structure around the reader's task. For internal updates, newsletters, FAQs, or incidents, use [internal communication formats](references/internal-comms.md).
3. Draft the substantive or uncertain section first. Explain the proposal, evidence, alternatives, tradeoffs, implementation, and success criteria as relevant. Write the summary once these are stable.
4. Revise focused passages without discarding accepted wording. Replace repetition, vague claims, unexplained terms, and unsupported commitments with precise text. Preserve conditions and uncertainty when shortening.
5. Check comprehension using only the draft and linked sources. Can a new reader explain what changes, why, who acts, what happens next, and what remains undecided? Repair contradictions and conversation-only references.

Use an independent reader only if one is available and authorized. Otherwise label the review as self-review; a fresh paid model session is not required.

## Completion

Return the complete draft and the few substantive questions still open. Do not turn missing owners, dates, policies, or results into invented facts. For a small edit, apply the edit directly rather than imposing a full coauthoring process. Creating a draft does not itself authorize sending or publishing it.

Example: “Turn these notes into a proposal to replace the support queue.” Deliver a proposal with the current problem, options, recommended change, transition responsibilities, measures of success, and clearly marked decisions still needed. If the notes lack a budget, identify that gap rather than assigning a plausible cost.
