# Reference, structure, and staged evidence

Start from visible evidence: outline, major masses, negative space, part junctions, repeated details, surface character, and lighting. Use a crop for a disputed detail instead of repeatedly inspecting a large unchanged image. Record hidden-side assumptions explicitly.

A single image provides projective ratios, not absolute dimensions. Establish physical scale only from supplied measurements or a known reference. Read the scene's unit settings before converting dimensions. Use one coordinate convention and convert at boundaries; never mix pixel lengths and millimeters without calibration.

Match the camera, crop, and projection before judging geometry. Compare silhouette and landmark placement under neutral materials first. Build macro structure before small details: a bevel cannot repair the wrong overall proportions. Keep semantic parts and attachment points separate when they must move. For a repeated pattern, use a parameterized generator instead of manually duplicating inconsistent elements.

For Blender headless work, prefer data/bmesh operations; context-sensitive operators need a focused proof in that environment. Preserve a source scene before destructive operations. For multi-part construction, establish a shared units/interface spec; independently authored pieces need a final assembly check even when each piece passes its own tests. Parallel builders are optional and require authorization.

Review the same camera at the same resolution when using image differences or silhouette overlap. Such metrics detect a particular mismatch; they do not establish material fidelity, hidden geometry, topology, or motion readiness. Verify a fitted model through another view or measurement rather than only its optimization target. Keep pass, fail, and unavailable/degraded evidence distinct.

Use the smallest correction that addresses the visible defect. Stop an unproductive correction loop to report the missing evidence or renderer limitation; do not invent a pass. Texture projection needs rights to the image and attention to baked lighting and unseen surfaces. A textured front plane is not a complete reconstructed model.
