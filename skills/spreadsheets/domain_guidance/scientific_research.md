# Research observations and analysis

Preserve the recorded measurements and the study's stated analysis plan. Establish what one row represents, the variable definitions, measurement units, sampling times, and treatment of missing or censored observations before constructing calculations. Do not change a protocol to make a result easier to present.

Use stable record identifiers. Keep analytical fields machine-readable: typed values in rectangular columns, explicit quality flags, and a documented codebook. Colour and comments can supplement a flag but cannot be its only representation in data intended for export.

Store corrections and transformations so they can be traced to their inputs. Document exclusion rules and the resulting sample count. Distinguish repeated observations from independent units; the appropriate analysis depends on the study design. A tidy worksheet is not evidence that a statistical method is valid.

When using formulas, retain their assumptions and verify representative results independently. For randomization or simulation, record the method, seed where supported, and relevant software version; volatile worksheet functions may change on recalculation. Preserve a reproducible result and the requested editable calculation separately when needed.

Report the checks actually performed and any missing protocol or source detail. Use [local recalculation guidance](../local.md#recalculate-and-verify) for workbook mechanics. This guide addresses data handling, not the scientific validity of a study's conclusions.
