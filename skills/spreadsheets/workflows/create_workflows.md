# Creating a new spreadsheet

## Quality bar

- The workbook must be correct, readable, and auditable for its audience — and simple to keep updated.
- Before filling anything in, identify from the request, any reference/template, and applicable domain guidance: the audience, the decision at hand, the target engine, outputs, inputs, calculations, and checks. Roles do not force tabs — combine compatible roles, and add tabs only for distinct readers, dependencies, refresh boundaries, auditability, or an explicit request.
- Keep related inputs and calculation steps together. Compute each result in exactly one place and link other views to it. A small supporting block that fits an existing sheet does not get its own tab.
- Requested insights or recommendations appear once, on the main dashboard, cover, or summary (or beside the relevant results when none exists), and are supported by figures or rules already shown. Skip generic rationale, invented scoring, and long formula-generated narrative; keep genuinely useful short calculated statuses and actions.
- For triage, priorities, or next actions, make the records needing attention — and why — easy to find, using formatting as the visual cue. Owners, actions, and deadlines belong when relevant and supported by the data. Summary counts must trace back to those records.
- After autofit, pull oversized widths/heights back down without clipping content.

## Checks

- A dedicated "Checks" tab is not for simple spreadsheets; add one only when the task's complexity makes it useful.
